# Product and UI Proposal

## Executive Proposal

Evolve Axiom into an **AI operating company with a productizable control plane**.

The near-term objective would not be to prove that agents can imitate a corporate org chart or start many unrelated businesses. It would be to prove that one founder can govern a real, revenue-producing business through a small number of evidence-backed decisions while Axiom performs increasingly more of the work within explicit boundaries.

The proposed sequence is:

1. Axiom uses its own operating system to run one narrow business.
2. That business produces real customers, revenue, operating decisions, failures, and learning.
3. Repeated low-risk work earns greater autonomy and consumes less Founder attention.
4. A second related business tests whether the operating capabilities transfer.
5. Only proven capabilities are considered for an external software product or broader platform.

This keeps the long-term promise — **many businesses, one intelligence** — while giving it a smaller and more credible path to evidence.

## Proposed Company Evolution

### Sequence the Current Identities

Axiom currently contains three compatible but easily confused ideas:

| Identity | Immediate role | Proposed sequencing |
|----------|----------------|---------------------|
| AI operating company | Builds and operates Axiom-owned or Axiom-managed businesses | Primary company identity |
| Vertical DFY business | Produces near-term customer outcomes and revenue | First commercial proof |
| Founder operating system | Helps one person govern agents, decisions, permissions, and economics | Internal product first; external product later |

The proposal is to sequence these identities instead of presenting all three as equally mature today.

The immediate user of the operating system would remain the Founding Partner. The first external customer would buy a concrete vertical outcome, not a general agent platform. An external founder product would be considered only after internal operating proof exists.

### Build Related Business Cells, Not a Random Conglomerate

New businesses should initially share a common operating machine. One candidate machine is:

```
Detect revenue leakage
        |
Gather evidence and estimate impact
        |
Recommend and implement a fix
        |
Measure recovered revenue
        |
Improve and transfer the playbook
```

The same core capabilities could support law firms with intake leakage, HVAC companies missing after-hours calls, dental practices losing unbooked inquiries, or other workflow-heavy businesses.

This approach creates compounding value. Each new business tests and improves reusable acquisition, intake, follow-up, attribution, experimentation, and reporting capabilities rather than starting from zero.

### Business Cell

Consider evolving the Company Vertical concept into a more operational **Business Cell** model. This is a product concept for discussion, not a proposed documentation rename yet.

Each cell would have:

- Customer, problem, offer, and pricing
- Operating thesis and critical assumptions
- One primary business outcome
- Budget, runway, and Founder-attention allocation
- Approved tools and permission boundaries
- Current experiments and active runs
- Independent P&L
- Scale, pivot, and kill criteria
- Lessons eligible for promotion into the shared factory

Example mandate:

> Generate three qualified sales conversations per month for law-firm intake services. The cell may research, score, draft, and prepare campaigns autonomously. External messages, client-data access, and spend remain gated until the relevant capability earns a broader permission envelope.

## Proposed Product Principles

### 1. Outcomes Over Activity

The system should report business results, exceptions, and decisions rather than emphasizing how many agents were active or how many tasks they completed.

### 2. Evidence Before Authority

Recommendations should show their evidence. Greater autonomy should be earned through repeated, measured performance rather than granted because an agent occupies a senior role.

### 3. Founder Attention Is Capital

Founder time should be measured alongside money and token spend. A profitable business that requires constant review may be less scalable than a smaller business that operates reliably with minimal intervention.

### 4. Deterministic Control, Agentic Judgment

Agents are most useful for research, synthesis, drafting, classification, and handling ambiguous inputs. Budgets, permissions, approvals, schedules, idempotency, ledgers, and external execution should be enforced by deterministic systems.

### 5. Learn From Outcomes, Not Memory Alone

Useful organizational memory should connect a hypothesis, decision, action, result, and policy change. A large store of agent conversations is not, by itself, institutional learning.

## Proposed Product Capabilities

### Founder Command Center

The primary product surface should be a decision and operating console rather than an open-ended chat interface.

A useful daily view could summarize:

- Three decisions requiring Founder judgment
- Material risks or exceptions
- Active business cells and outcome movement
- Changes in revenue, cost, pipeline, and Founder attention
- Experiments that completed or changed direction
- Actions executed automatically within policy
- Permissions that may be ready for promotion or demotion

Conversation with the General Secretary can remain available, but it should support the command surface rather than become the only way to understand the company.

### Outcome Contracts

Every significant mission should begin with a structured outcome contract.

| Field | Purpose |
|-------|---------|
| Outcome | The business result being pursued |
| Metric | How success will be measured |
| Deadline | When the result or decision is due |
| Budget | Maximum financial and compute cost |
| Evidence standard | What must support findings or recommendations |
| Permission envelope | What can be done without further approval |
| Stop conditions | When work must halt |
| Escalation conditions | When Founder or specialist judgment is required |
| Acceptance criteria | What makes the mission complete |

Example:

> Identify 25 law firms with observable intake leakage, produce five evidence-backed diagnostic briefs, and prepare five personalized outreach messages. Maximum tool cost: $40. Do not contact firms. Every material claim must cite public evidence.

### Founder Decision Queue

The existing gate-card concept could become a richer decision product. Each card should contain:

- Exact decision required
- Recommended choice
- Expected upside and cost
- Risks and blast radius
- Confidence and supporting evidence
- Reversibility and rollback
- What happens if no action is taken
- Exact external payload or state change that approval would trigger
- Expiration date

Available decisions could include:

- Approve once
- Approve with edits
- Reject with reason
- Request a smaller experiment
- Grant a bounded rule for similar future actions

The system should later compare the recommendation with the actual outcome. This creates a record of decision quality, not only decision history.

### Earned Autonomy

Autonomy should be assigned per capability and action type, not per agent personality.

| Level | Capability |
|-------|------------|
| 1 — Observe | Analyze and record; do not recommend or act |
| 2 — Draft | Prepare artifacts for review |
| 3 — Recommend | Propose a specific action with evidence |
| 4 — Execute with approval | Execute the exact approved payload |
| 5 — Execute within an envelope | Act automatically inside defined limits |
| 6 — Manage exceptions | Operate the workflow and escalate anomalies only |

Promotion would depend on evaluation history, outcome quality, reversibility, and risk. Capabilities could also be demoted after failure.

### Run Replay and Evidence

Every material run should be inspectable as a business narrative:

```
Objective -> Plan -> Tasks -> Evidence -> Review -> Approval -> Action -> Outcome -> Lesson
```

A Founder should be able to ask “Why did Axiom recommend this?” and inspect:

- Sources and captured observations
- Facts, inferences, and assumptions
- Scoring criteria
- Intermediate artifacts
- Review and evaluation results
- Differences between proposed and approved actions
- Measured outcome after execution

### Experiment and Learning Ledger

The system should record:

- Hypothesis
- Cheapest credible experiment
- Expected result
- Actual result
- Reason for the difference
- Decision taken
- Playbook or policy changed
- Whether the lesson is local to one cell or transferable

This would make failure useful and prevent “continuous improvement” from remaining an unmeasured claim.

### Founder-Attention Accounting

Candidate metrics include:

- Founder minutes consumed per business cell
- Number and quality of escalations
- Approval turnaround time
- Rework requested
- Revenue or learning generated per Founder hour
- Percentage of escalations later judged unnecessary

### Portfolio Capital Allocation

This is a later-stage capability. When multiple cells have real operating data, Axiom could recommend whether to fund, continue, constrain, pivot, or kill each cell based on:

- Evidence strength
- Revenue and margin
- Growth potential
- Founder attention
- Capital required
- Risk
- Transferable learning value

Building this before multiple cells have real economics would create portfolio theater rather than useful decision support.

## Candidate First End-to-End Proof

The current law-firm acquisition motion provides a concrete candidate because the repository already defines its market, outreach sequence, report contract, packages, and approval requirements.

One bounded operating loop could be:

1. Select one ZIP and a narrow operating objective.
2. Enumerate candidate firms from public sources.
3. Evaluate observable intake and conversion leakage using a fixed rubric.
4. Attach evidence to every material finding.
5. Rank firms by opportunity and fit.
6. Produce a short diagnostic artifact.
7. Draft personalized outreach.
8. Run factual, brand, and compliance review.
9. Present the exact outreach batch for Founder approval.
10. Send through a deterministic delivery system.
11. Track replies, calls, proposals, and revenue.
12. Recommend the next experiment using measured results.

The proof is not that an agent can write a report. The proof is that Axiom can operate the complete loop, remain inside its authority, produce a commercial result, and improve the next run.

Law should remain a candidate rather than an assumed first wedge until the owner confirms customer access, domain credibility, risk tolerance, and the desired 90-day proof. A home-services lead-recovery loop may offer faster feedback and lower domain risk while reusing much of the same machinery.

## Agentic Organization as a Product

The corporate metaphor remains useful when it clarifies accountability. It becomes less useful when it produces permanent roles, meetings, or simulated hierarchy without improving an outcome.

### Proposed Mapping

| Existing concept | Product manifestation |
|------------------|-----------------------|
| Founding Partner | Final authority, capital allocator, and exception handler |
| General Secretary | Command layer that synthesizes state, routes work, and maintains the decision queue |
| Think Tank | On-demand review capabilities invoked by mission risk and content |
| C-Suite officer | Specialized capability or policy review, not necessarily a persistent persona |
| Company Vertical | Ring-fenced business cell with a mandate, economics, permissions, and lifecycle |
| Agent | Worker executing a bounded task under an outcome contract |
| Meeting | Structured artifact review or decision event |
| Promotion / trust | Broader permission envelope earned from measured performance |

Examples:

- A CFO capability is invoked for pricing, spend, unit economics, or material financial risk.
- A CLO capability reviews legal exposure, outreach compliance, and binding actions.
- A research capability can serve multiple cells but receives cell-scoped context and evidence requirements.
- A CPO-like function owns a cell mandate and outcomes without requiring a simulated daily management hierarchy.

### Concepts to Strengthen

- Approval gates as visible, enforceable permission policy
- Per-cell profitability and kill criteria
- Async artifacts and written decisions
- Cross-cell reuse of proven capabilities
- Fail-closed behavior for high-risk external actions
- The centralized decision queue

### Concepts to Simplify or Reconsider

- Persistent executive personas for every function
- One permanent agent for each corporate title
- Recurring meetings and scrum simulations
- Automatic agent spawning as the primary scaling mechanism
- Open access for every agent to interrupt the Founder
- Measuring organizational maturity by agent count

The proposed replacement is a smaller set of capabilities invoked by outcome contracts, with explicit escalation and capacity rules.

## Website and Interface Proposal

### Current Strengths

The current website has a distinctive visual language: dark surfaces, bright modular cards, orbital imagery, compact labels, and a strong “Many businesses. One intelligence.” idea. The law page is the clearest commercial surface because it names recognizable customer problems, packages, and prices.

### Current Product Communication Gap

The homepage primarily presents a polished concept poster. It describes the Founder, General Secretary, Think Tank, verticals, profitability, and asynchronous work, but it does not yet let a visitor observe the system operating.

The broad DFY page also weakens differentiation when it presents dozens of services and industries at once. Claims such as “live,” “infinite capacity,” “10x faster,” or broad service counts should either link to evidence or be replaced with demonstrated operating metrics.

### Homepage Direction

Keep the long-term line:

> Many businesses. One intelligence.

Ground it immediately with a product explanation such as:

> One founder governing AI-operated businesses through evidence, budgets, and explicit authority.

Candidate primary calls to action:

- **Watch a business run**
- **Enter the command center**
- **See the operating log**

“Read the factory brief” can remain a secondary action.

### Turn the Bento Grid Into a Product Surface

The existing visual system can display real operating state instead of only conceptual labels:

- Active business cell and current mandate
- Decisions waiting for the Founder
- Current outcome contract
- Latest approved external action
- Revenue, cost, and pipeline movement
- Founder attention this week
- Current experiment and expected result
- Autonomy recently earned or revoked
- Latest failed hypothesis and resulting change

Until the data is real, any example should be labeled **Prototype**, **Simulation**, or **Sample run**. “Live” should link to meaningful live activity.

### Proposed Product Screens and Public Demonstrations

| Surface | Purpose | Candidate content |
|---------|---------|-------------------|
| `/command` | Founder command center | Morning brief, decision queue, risks, active cells, economics, attention |
| `/ventures` | Portfolio view | Cell status, mandate, primary metric, latest result, fund/pivot/kill state |
| `/ventures/{slug}` | Business-cell detail | Charter, P&L, permission envelope, experiments, active runs, lessons |
| `/runs/{id}` | Run replay | Timeline, evidence, reviews, approval, action, outcome |
| `/decisions` | Governance history | Recommendation, evidence, verdict, forecast vs. actual |
| `/proof` | Commercial evidence | Case studies, measured outcomes, cost, Founder time, failures |
| `/operating-log` | Sanitized company history | Launches, decisions, revenue events, policy changes, lessons |
| `/lab` | Interactive demonstration | Turn a rough business idea into assumptions, experiments, and an outcome contract |

Public pages would use sanitized or explicitly simulated data. The private command center would retain client-sensitive evidence, exact financial records, and approval controls.

### High-Value Interactions

1. **Inspect the evidence** — click any recommendation or claim to open its source drawer.
2. **Watch a run** — replay a mission from objective to measured outcome.
3. **Approve the exact action** — review the email, spend, deployment, or state change rather than approving an abstract plan.
4. **Grant bounded autonomy** — promote a capability from approval-required to a capped permission envelope.
5. **Ask why** — query why a segment, offer, or action was selected and receive an answer linked to decisions and experiments.
6. **Compare forecast with actual** — see where Axiom's expected result differed from reality.
7. **Launch a cell** — enter a rough idea and receive multiple operating theses, assumptions, and cheapest experiments instead of a generic business plan.
8. **Allocate capital** — later, explore how money and Founder attention change portfolio recommendations.

### Candidate Command Center Layout

```
+-------------------------------------------------------------+
| Morning brief: 3 decisions | 2 risks | 1 opportunity       |
+----------------------------+--------------------------------+
| Founder decision queue     | Active business cells          |
| - exact action             | - primary outcome              |
| - evidence                 | - current experiment           |
| - approve / revise / stop  | - autonomy level               |
+----------------------------+--------------------------------+
| Revenue and cost delta     | Founder attention              |
+----------------------------+--------------------------------+
| Latest run replay          | Lessons and policy changes     |
+-------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Connect Every UI Surface to Operating State

| UI element | Underlying system concept |
|------------|---------------------------|
| Business-cell card | Mandate, primary outcome, ledger, lifecycle state |
| Decision card | Gate card, evidence, exact payload, expiry, verdict |
| Autonomy badge | Enforced permission policy and evaluation history |
| Run timeline | Durable execution events and artifacts |
| Evidence drawer | Source provenance, observations, assumptions, confidence |
| Forecast vs. actual | Recommendation record joined to measured outcome |
| Founder-attention chart | Review time, escalations, rework, intervention count |
| Learning entry | Experiment result connected to a policy or playbook change |
| Portfolio view | Cell economics, attention, risk, and allocation decision |

This connection is important: the website should not merely visualize an agent organization. It should expose the records and controls that make the organization governable.

### Brand and Offer Structure

Consider separating the company story from individual vertical offers:

- **Axiom Intelligence** — the operating company and control system
- **Axiom-operated vertical brands or offer pages** — customer-facing outcomes such as law-firm intake or home-services lead recovery

This would let vertical customers receive a clear, focused proposition without requiring them to understand the internal factory. It would also let the Axiom site demonstrate the operating model without becoming a generic agency catalog.

## Ideas for Future Roadmap Review

The roadmap could be organized around demonstrated capability moments rather than organizational or infrastructure completion.

| Stage | Demonstration |
|-------|---------------|
| 0 — Owner decision | Company identity, first wedge, proof metric, and authority boundary are chosen |
| 1 — First Founder Morning | Command Center produces a useful daily brief, even if work behind it is partly manual |
| 2 — First Controlled Run | One bounded mission completes with evidence, review, and a durable record |
| 3 — First External Action | An exact Founder-approved action reaches a real prospect or customer |
| 4 — First Dollar | A tracked run contributes to revenue |
| 5 — First Learned Improvement | Outcome data changes the next run or policy |
| 6 — First Earned Permission | A repeated low-risk action moves into a bounded autonomous envelope |
| 7 — First Low-Touch Week | The business operates under a defined Founder-attention threshold |
| 8 — Second Workflow | A second workflow succeeds inside the same business cell |
| 9 — First Transfer | A proven capability reduces time, cost, or uncertainty in a second related cell |
| 10 — Productization Decision | Evidence determines whether the control plane should become external software |

### Candidate Proof Measures

The owner should select a small set rather than adopting all of them:

- Paying customers and revenue influenced
- Founder minutes per operating cycle
- Percentage of runs passing evaluation without material rework
- Unauthorized or out-of-policy actions
- Decision forecast accuracy
- Cost per qualified opportunity or customer outcome
- Percentage of work completed inside earned permission envelopes
- Improvement from one run to the next
- Time or cost saved when transferring a capability to another cell

## What Not to Build Yet

The following ideas may become valuable later but appear premature before one closed commercial loop works:

- General-purpose external agent platform
- Agent or workflow marketplace
- Self-spawning autonomous organizations
- Elaborate graphical org chart
- Rich cross-business memory or knowledge graph without measured learning records
- Dozens of integrations without a proven workflow
- Fully autonomous spend, deployment, or customer outreach
- Enterprise multi-user collaboration
- Forty simultaneous industry offerings
- Sophisticated portfolio optimization before multiple cells have real economics
- A second business that does not reuse the first business's operating machinery

A candidate rule for new infrastructure:

> It must close the current revenue loop, reduce Founder attention, improve decision quality, strengthen control, or make a proven capability transferable.

## Owner Decisions Required

### Company and Business Model

1. Should Axiom primarily own businesses and their profits, sell managed outcomes, sell software subscriptions, or pursue a deliberate sequence of these models?
2. Should the portfolio initially contain related businesses sharing one operating machine, or is operating unrelated businesses essential to the thesis?
3. Should vertical offers remain under Axiom or become distinct Axiom-operated brands?

### First Proof

4. Is law genuinely the first operating wedge, based on customer access and domain credibility, or is it currently the most developed offer?
5. Would home-services lead recovery provide a faster or safer first closed loop?
6. What single result would count as meaningful proof in the next 90 days: a paying customer, an autonomous external action, reduced Founder time, repeated weekly operation, or another outcome?

### Founder Experience and Authority

7. Should the primary Founder experience be a concise decision queue, a conversational General Secretary, or both?
8. Which actions, if any, may become autonomous first: research, drafting, website changes, outreach, spending, customer responses, or deployment?
9. What is the acceptable daily or weekly Founder-attention budget for one proven cell?

### Product Story and Transparency

10. How much of the agent-company and C-Suite metaphor is essential to the desired product experience?
11. Should Axiom publicly expose sanitized experiments, failures, economics, decisions, and operating history?
12. Should “live” public surfaces show production data, delayed sanitized data, or only clearly labeled demonstrations?

## Review Outcomes

Owner review should produce decisions on four points before this proposal influences the approved vision or roadmap:

1. **Identity** — what Axiom is first
2. **Wedge** — which business and operating loop will prove it
3. **Proof** — what measurable outcome validates the thesis
4. **Authority** — what the system may do without the Founder

If approved in whole or in part, those decisions should be recorded in [decision_log.md](decision_log.md) before changes are made to the charter, organization, lifecycle, operations, or implementation roadmap.
